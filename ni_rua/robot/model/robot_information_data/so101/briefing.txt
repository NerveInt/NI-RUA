SO-101 physical reference

Scope: static physical knowledge for the installed SO-101 follower arm. This is not a live observation, manufacturer safety specification, or task solution. Runtime robot_capabilities, current measured proprioception and the current tool contracts take precedence. The original URDF is supplied inline, in metres, radians and kilograms; the compact reference adds interface and control conventions without duplicating its joint or inertial records. Live camera calibration comes from observations, never from this text.

Your body is one fixed-base TheRobotStudio SO-101 follower arm. It has five serial revolute arm joints, in order shoulder_pan, shoulder_lift, elbow_flex, wrist_flex, wrist_roll, followed by a single revolute gripper jaw joint named gripper that swings one moving jaw against a fixed jaw. Each joint is driven by a Feetech STS3215 serial bus servo in position control with a 12-bit encoder (4096 ticks per revolution, about 0.0015 rad per tick). The native action is six values: five arm joint angles plus one gripper command. The only public arm slot is right; there is no left arm and requests for it are rejected.

Frame so101_base is the URDF base_link, with +Z up when the base is mounted flat. In this deployment world equals the robot base. All URDF joint axes are local +Z of their joint frames; apply each parent-to-joint transform before joint motion, because local axis signs alone are not world directions. Joint zero follows the URDF new-calibration convention: zero is the middle of each joint's calibrated range, not a fully extended pose. At all arm joints zero the TCP is about (0.391, 0.000, 0.227) m in so101_base, ahead of and above the base. The LeRobot driver reports arm joints in degrees centred on its recorded calibration midpoint; the backend converts them to URDF radians with the deployment's per-joint sign and zero offset (joint_mapping), which the deployment's calibration sets for the installed unit, and maps the gripper command linearly onto the deployment's closed and open jaw angles. This reference does not establish those per-unit values; measured joint angles and the published calibration residuals do.

The TCP is URDF link gripper_frame_link, fixed to gripper_link: a point on the fixed jaw's inner fingertip face, not between the jaws. Cartesian poses are position_m in metres plus quaternion_xyzw of gripper_frame_link in so101_base. Named angles_deg poses are not supported. Local +Z of gripper_frame_link points outward along the jaws (approach direction) and local +X from the jaw gap toward the fixed jaw; the wrist camera is on local +Y. The whole jaw gap lies on local -X: the moving jaw swings about local -Y, opening moves its tip along local -X and, at wide openings, back behind the TCP plane, so the midpoint between the fingertips moves with the jaw angle (CAD: 7.9 mm along -X at jaw 0 rad). The TCP is a geometric point, not a report that an object is held.

With five arm joints, an arbitrary 6D TCP pose is generally not reachable: at a given position the approach axis (local +Z) can tilt only within the arm's vertical plane through shoulder_pan, while the roll about the approach axis comes from wrist_roll within its joint limits. A straight-down approach is therefore reachable at every yaw except a band that the wrist_roll limits exclude; the motion instructions state it with its derivation. IK minimises a weighted position and orientation error and the receipt reports the achieved position and orientation residuals; stage tolerances decide completed versus tracking_error, and are not silently widened. A failed local IK solve does not prove global unreachability; a successful plan does not prove physically completed motion or grasp retention. URDF joint limits are nominal model bounds, not verified hardware safety limits. Wrist rotation and flexion move the jaws and the wrist camera through space.

Gripper command goal_normalized is dimensionless: 0 requests closed, 1 requests open, mapped linearly onto the jaw joint angles configured as closed and open in the deployment, within the declared gripper limits of -0.1745 to 1.7453 rad. A requested jaw angle is not a measured angle. Measured jaw position is reported separately; an object blocking closure, servo torque limiting or calibration error leave it away from the target, and it is not a grasp-success, contact or force signal. The driver limits the gripper servo torque to reduce burnout; usable grasp force is unknown.

The wrist camera (a 32x32 mm UVC module in a printed mount) is fixed to gripper_link and protrudes beside the jaws; the URDF includes its mount and housing meshes. The URDF housing link is not the optical frame: wrist camera intrinsics and T_link_camera, and the fixed camera's T_base_camera, come from the deployment calibration through observations. Without calibration those values are unavailable rather than guessed. Collision elements reuse the visual meshes; no planner collision spheres exist, and clearance of the elbow, wrist, camera mount, jaws or a held object is not checked by this reference.

Timing is real time. The world keeps evolving while you reason and nothing is paused. Each dispatched command is a joint position goal; the step counter counts dispatched commands, not simulated time. Maximum joint speed and per-command joint delta are deployment settings reported by runtime capabilities. Servo position tracking can lag, stall under load or stop short against contact. Stop holds the present measured position and is confirmed only when measured velocity settles; otherwise stop status is unknown. Reset moves only the arm to a recorded home joint configuration and never restores objects.

After motion, use actual measured joint state and fresh permitted camera images to distinguish requested, planned, commanded and achieved motion, and to distinguish empty closure, contact, a retained grasp and release. The URDF link masses sum to about 0.667 kg and are CAD estimates with placeholder camera masses. No payload rating, grasp force, stall torque, friction, gear backlash, compliance, repeatability or stopping response is established by this reference.
